Q1 — Who's buying?

Three channels do 70% of the work.

Organic remains our biggest channel, but paid_search is the fastest-growing — and the most expensive per dollar earned.

Email is punching above its weight: small audience, but the highest repeat rate.

Decision implication: the email list is a leverage point. If we doubled it we'd likely add more margin than doubling paid_search.

This is the classic head-and-tail pattern. Most retailers see it.

What's noteworthy: 23 of our 120 SKUs sold fewer than 5 units in 60 days. Together they hold $48,000 in working capital.

Every dollar tied up in a slow mover is a dollar we couldn't use to restock a hero SKU.

Q3 — Who comes back?

Our Q1 cohort retains better than the Q3 cohort.

Q4 — What about the leaks?

3 in 10 shoppers fill a cart and walk away.

Views ████████████████████ 100% Add to cart █████ 19% Checkout ███ 13% Purchase ██ 9%

The biggest drop is add-to-cart → checkout: customers picked the item but didn't start paying.

This funnel is the most actionable chart we have. A 1-percentage-point improvement here is worth ~$35k/month.

Mobile abandonment is 8 points higher than desktop. That's where to start.

Our model — trained on 18 months of daily sales — projects the next 30 days at $420k ± 50k in revenue.

The model accounts for: weekly cycles (weekends are 30% higher), seasonality, and recent growth trend. It does not know about: promotions we haven't run yet, new product launches, or external shocks. Use the band, not the point.

If we did one thing

Mobile checkout is the lever.

Same products. Same prices. The mobile shopper completes 8 points less often than desktop. If we close half that gap, we add roughly $50,000 per month in revenue at zero additional ad spend.

No new spend. No new SKUs. Just a checkout that doesn't lose people on the second tap.
